ESOP Explainer

What are ESOPs?

Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOPs) are a benefit that allows employees to own a share of the company's future growth. When the company's value increases, your options become more valuable.

How ESOPs Work

1

Grant

You receive stock options at a set price (strike price)

2

Vesting

Your options gradually become yours (usually 4 years)

3

Exercise

Once vested, you can buy shares at the strike price

4

Profit

If company value grows, you benefit from the appreciation

Example: 4-Year Vesting

Year 1 (Cliff) 25% vested
Year 2 50% vested
Year 3 75% vested
Year 4 100% vested

Tax Implications

At Grant

No tax when options are granted

At Exercise

Tax depends on difference between strike price and current valuation

At Sale

Capital gains tax applies on profit when you sell shares

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the company is acquired? expand_more

Options typically become exercisable if an acquisition occurs. The acquirer may also offer to buy your options or convert them to their options.

Can I lose my options? expand_more

Unvested options are forfeited if you leave the company. Most companies give 30-90 days to exercise vested options after resignation.

What's the strike price? expand_more

The price at which you can buy the stock. Usually set at the company's valuation at the time of grant. If company value grows, you profit.

Can I trade my options? expand_more

Typically not on a public market until the company goes public (IPO) or is acquired. Private company options are hard to trade.
